Problem statement

How can public threat context be converted into documented risks, control objectives, test cases, findings and management recommendations, while preserving traceability and distinguishing verified facts, assumptions and simulated results?

Objectives

  • Record public threat claims with source, date, verification status and limitations.
  • Develop clearly labeled hypothetical banking cyber-risk scenarios.
  • Map risks to control objectives and relevant frameworks/regulatory sources.
  • Demonstrate selected control tests using synthetic data and tabletop exercises.
  • Document findings, remediation, retest outcomes and unresolved governance gaps.
  • Produce executive reporting, KPI scorecard, dashboard and navigable evidence trail.

Scope and exclusions

Included: public-source context; three illustrative risks; conceptual NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 mapping; preliminary OJK/BI regulatory source review; synthetic MFA and login tests; ransomware and fraud-escalation tabletop artifacts; draft privacy/vendor assessments; incident response playbook; reporting and QA artifacts.

Excluded: production access, customer data, real penetration testing, actual vendor due diligence, confirmation or attribution of a real incident, verified control effectiveness at a bank, legal opinion, audit assurance and certification.

Methodology and traceability

Public evidence (EV) → hypothetical risk (CR) → control objective (CTRL) → test (TEST) → finding (FIND) → remediation/retest → executive results/KPI. Links demonstrate documentary traceability only, not operational effectiveness at a real institution.

07 · Project conclusion & lessons learned

Conclusion. Trustify successfully demonstrates the design and documentation of a structured, evidence-traceable GRC assessment workflow using public-source intelligence and synthetic banking scenarios. The work produced a successful synthetic MFA remediation retest and two expected login-detection alerts. It also transparently records unresolved recovery, escalation, regulatory, risk-treatment and independent-review limitations.

What this project demonstrates

  • Ability to translate a broad cybersecurity issue into a defined assessment scope and method.
  • Ability to connect evidence, risks, control objectives, tests, findings and management reporting.
  • Ability to preserve failed baseline results and document a separate remediation retest.
  • Ability to distinguish a successful synthetic test from operational effectiveness and legal compliance.
  • Ability to communicate open issues and required closure evidence rather than overstating project success.

What remains outside the achieved result

  • Residual risk and treatment effectiveness have not been assessed at organizational level.
  • Recovery and escalation governance findings remain open.
  • Regulatory clauses and entity/activity applicability require detailed verification.
  • Independent quality assurance has not been performed.

The project documentation baseline is consolidated. The underlying assessment is not fully closed and should not be described as a completed audit or certification.
